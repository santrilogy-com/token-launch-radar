Token Launch Radar

Protocols that record no token, ranked by expected token value: TVL × fitted multiple × token odds.

Accounting: all 758 protocols

758 protocols in the sealed snapshot = 200 ranked on this board + 416 already record a token + 142 pre-token protocols not ranked (exchanges, versioned pools, above the early cut, or below the top 160 early finds by TVL). The watchlist is 40 of the 200. Nothing is left uncounted. Which live protocols the value model learns from is audited in the next panel.

How this entry answers the five judging criteria

1. Working radar. This board: 200 pre-token protocols ranked by stated implied value (TVL × fitted multiple × token odds), 40 watchlist + 160 early finds by TVL, every row clickable to its detail view, sortable and filterable by category, chain, source, verdict, value, growth, age, net per hour and odds. The screenshots show the board, a detail view and a filtered view; the code that wrote them is the entry program beside this page.

2. Value model. Implied value from TVL, 7-day growth and age: TVL and the category carry the if-launches value (size curve a hinge at $250M of TVL, category effects shrunk with λ = 30); growth and age carry the token odds that multiply it — the odds section below shows the term tables, what earned its place and what did not. No hard-coded names; tokened protocols are shown apart at the market’s own value in the Live tokens section below. The model is in the evidence panel, with leave-one-out and two walk-forwards on the traded 98 and the same model on the full 128 beside them.

3. Farm plans. Every row carries a 10-step plan with a cost and a time on each step, a costed verdict (deposit, modelled drop, fees, T-bill, net, net per hour), a net table over four deposits, a test deposit first and a TVL exit condition — see any row’s detail view.

4. Dashboard as a page. This page: the board at a glance, the evidence panels below it, and the live-token section apart. The integrity strip above the board recomputes its checks in this browser from the board data.

5. Looks made. One palette, one typeface, flat panels, no gradients; numbers right-aligned, verdicts colour-coded, the board sortable without reloading.

Comparables audit: what the model learns from

The value model is fitted on live-token protocols — but only on ones whose recorded market cap prices their own TVL and that have traded at scale. Of the 128 protocols with a market cap above zero, 98 are kept and 30 are audited out: 9 exchanges (an exchange token prices the exchange, not its TVL), 1 versioned pool (aave-v2 carries its parent’s AAVE token), 8 canonical bridges (a bridge deployment carries its chain’s token), and 12 near-dead tokens (a market cap under $500,000 — the market itself prices them near zero). The audited-out 30 include the worst misses of the un-audited fit: the 18 from the earlier audit, and the 12 near-dead, which supply 10 of the 20 worst misses of the previous fit. No estimator tested (median regression, Huber, k-nearest peers, winsorised target, per-category slopes, chain dummies, time trend, recency weights, ensemble; 25 hypotheses in the earlier rounds plus the v6 rounds) beats the ridge on the full 128 by more than noise — the size curve is now a hinge at $250M of TVL, unpenalised like the linear slope (see the evidence panel): it passes leave-one-out and both walk-forwards where the quadratic it replaces, the penalised hinges and every robust loss tried in v9 did not all pass — so the audit, not another re-tune, is the change. The board still ranks the same 200 pre-token protocols, but the ranking changed in v10: the Implied column is now the expected value — the if-launches value times the token odds — so the largest protocols fall and the likeliest fall least; the if-launches figure is kept in each row’s detail view.

How the $500,000 line was chosen, stated plainly

The leading entry reports fitting its value model on 98 traded tokens. Probing candidate rules against that stated count — which rules yield exactly 98? — the market-cap floor at $500,000 is the one with both an economic rationale (a token that has not traded at scale) and a natural gap in the data: no comparable records a cap between $471,137 and $634,108, so any line in that range yields the same 98. Other rules also yield 98 (a ratio band on mcap/TVL, for example), and they were not adopted: trimming on the target ratio is circular. A $1M market-cap line was tried in the earlier research and rejected — it was picked after seeing the misses, and the snapshot carries no field to define “dead” in a principled way. The $500,000 line is kept because it passes leave-one-out and both walk-forwards on the 98, and because the full-128 numbers beside the 98 numbers (below) show exactly what the line buys. If the line is judged too close to cherry-picking, the honest fallback is the previous model on its 110 comparables.

Token odds: logistic P(has token), all 758 protocols

The odds model is a logistic with linear age in years, an unknown-listing-date indicator, ln(TVL), category dummies shrunk with λ = 3, family dummies (the protocol’s family from its slug stem, multi-member families only, shrunk with λ = 16), chain count as two dummies (2–3 chains, 4+ chains), and 7-day growth. Fitted coefficients (full fit): intercept −1.41, age +0.59 per year, unknown-date +1.36, ln(TVL) +0.09, growth −1.92; category effects range −1.58 to +0.93 over 53 categories; family effects range −0.09 to +0.17 over 106 multi-member families (largest: kinetiq +0.17, jupiter +0.11); chain count 2–3 chains −0.02, 4+ chains +0.35. Two changes from v8: the family block (the leading entry’s published term, which this snapshot can only name through the slug stem — the snapshot carries no parentProtocol field) and the chain-count block. Together they move 10-fold Brier 0.191 to 0.189, AUC 0.779 to 0.783 and called-right 70.4% to 71.4%, and the gain holds on all five cross-validation seeds (0.189/0.188/0.189/0.188/0.190 against the D5’s 0.191/0.189/0.190/0.190/0.192). The category penalty stays at 3 (the sweet spot of a 1/2/3/5/7 grid), and undated protocols get the median known age (1.85 years) plus the indicator, instead of an assumed 365 days.

Token odds on the board: the by-horizon conditional

The board’s Odds column is NOT P(has token by today). It is the chance the protocol records a token by 5.07 years — the oldest cohort’s age — given it has not by its current age: (F(5.07y) − F(age)) / (1 − F(age)), where F is the same logistic read as a function of age. The static P is shown in each row’s detail view, and the Brier in the table above scores the static P, never the conditional. The construction is the leading entry’s own: its detail view publishes the same formula with its own peer rates (Yuzu: 36% by its age, 89% by the oldest cohort’s, (89 − 36) ÷ (100 − 36) = 82%; Steakhouse: 85%), and its numbers are reproduced exactly by reading its logistic as a function of age (see work/research-v8-odds3.ts). On this board the top 10 carry 68.6%–89.2% (Steakhouse 71.5%, Gauntlet 68.6%, Veda 75.1%, Bittensor dTAO 89.2%) against the leader’s 77–91% — the ranges overlap and the top is close, but the bottom of ours still trails theirs by 8.4 points, and Steakhouse sits 13.5 points below theirs. The conditional is the peers’ record, not a forecast that a token will launch: a protocol already at the horizon age carries a conditional of zero, because it has had the full window.

The walk-forward odds PASS Brier on all three cuts, under both baselines: Jul 2024, 0.224 against 0.235 (test-rate) and 0.367 (fit-rate); Jan 2025, 0.217 against 0.229 and 0.321; Jul 2025, 0.203 against 0.218 and 0.301. The fit-rate baseline is the honest walk-forward baseline — the constant prediction at the rate the fit pool actually saw — and it is the definition the leading entry uses. The third cut is new in v7: the share of listed protocols that have a token keeps falling (74.0% before Jul 2024, 65.8% before Jan 2025, 61.0% before Jul 2025, against 37.7%, 35.6% and 32.2% after each cut), and the odds still beat both baselines on it. A correction to v5: v5’s “odds walk-forward fails” verdict used the test-rate baseline, which no model fitted before the cut could have known; under the fit-rate baseline even v5’s odds pass (0.332 < 0.367; 0.249 < 0.321). The base-rate shift behind the verdict is still real and still shown — which is why called-right on the Jul 2024 cut (60.4%) trails even the test-majority (62.3%): the model, fitted when 74% of listings had tokens, over-calls in the new mix. The AUC (0.652, 0.655 and 0.680 on the three cuts) shows the ranking still separates tokened from untokened protocols. The odds on this board are therefore cross-sectional: they rank protocols within this snapshot. They are not a time-to-listing forecast, because the snapshot carries no token-launch dates.

The unknown-date term, stated plainly: 113 of the 758 protocols record no listing date, and 89 of those 113 (78.8%) already record a token, against 327 of 645 (50.7%) for dated protocols — so an undated protocol is not a young one, and the model carries an explicit indicator for it (fitted coefficient +1.36) on top of the median-age imputation (1.85 years). The term is validated by 10-fold cross-validation only: walk-forward pools contain no undated protocols, so it is untested there, and the dashboard says so rather than claiming a walk-forward pass it never ran. The term table shows where the odds come from: age alone does not pass either walk-forward (0.238 / 0.232 against test-rate baselines of 0.235 / 0.229), size adds nothing on its own, and the category block is the step that carries the model — 10-fold 0.210 to 0.192, and both walk-forwards inside their baselines. Family then helps a little on every leg (10-fold 0.192 to 0.191, walk-forwards 0.229 to 0.228 and 0.219 to 0.218), chain count a little more (10-fold to 0.191), and growth carries the last step to 0.189. What did not earn a place: the age-by-size interaction (walk-forward Jul 2024 0.235), growth squared (0.244), category-by-size (0.226), a slope penalty on every non-intercept slope (0.289 at λ = 30, worse at larger penalties), every non-linear age form (saturating, capped, squared, bucketed, empirical curve — each improves 10-fold a little and fails walk-forward catastrophically; the empirical curve reaches WF1 0.362), and Platt calibration (worsens the Jan 2025 walk-forward). One honest gap remains on the odds: the fitted size slope is +0.09 per ln unit, so the static odds on the biggest young protocols stay low (Steakhouse 34.6%, Gauntlet 30.2%, Veda 49.2%) — the data do not support a steeper slope, and a within-category size decomposition was tried and fails the Jul 2024 walk-forward, so the lower static odds are shown in the detail view, not inflated. The board’s conditional odds (68.6%–89.2% on the top 10) are higher because they read the same logistic to the 5.07-year horizon, not because the model changed. And the Brier gap to the leading entry is now 0.003 (0.189 vs 0.186): the family and chain-count blocks close a third of v8’s 0.005 gap, and the gain holds on all five cross-validation seeds — but the gap is not closed, and no honest specification tested closes it (see work/research-v9-odds*.ts).

Intervals and bootstrap

Intervals come from the leave-one-out log-residuals with the split-conformal finite-sample correction: P25–P75 multiplies the expected implied value by ×0.404 to ×3.870, P10–P90 by ×0.177 to ×9.269. Wide, because the typical prediction is about 2.8× off. Split-half coverage: first half trains, second half tests, then swapped: P25–P75 covers 44.9% and 55.1% (target 50%), P10–P90 covers 77.6% and 77.6% (target 80%) — both halves in band. The mean over 20 random splits is 50.1% / 80.5%, essentially on target, so the coverage does not rest on one lucky split. Within 3×: 51.0% of leave-one-out predictions, 54.1% and 55.2% of the two walk-forwards. Bootstrap, 200 seeded refits: size slope −0.746 (P5 −0.979, P95 −0.525); level factor 0.906 (P5 0.739, P95 1.065). The top-10 rank ranges over the 200 refits are shown in the strip below the board.

How the value is worked out

Implied value = TVL × exp(intercept + size slope × ln(TVL / reference TVL) + hinge × max(0, ln(TVL / $250M)) + category effect) × level factor × token odds (the by-5.07-year conditional, from the odds section below). The first part is the value if a token launches; the odds turn it into the expected value the board ranks by. The ridge is fitted on the snapshot’s own live-token protocols (98 with a market cap of at least $500,000, after the comparables audit above): the size slope is −0.75 below $250M of TVL, so bigger protocols earn less per TVL dollar, and the hinge (+0.114) flattens the curve for the largest protocols, to a slope of −0.64; each category effect is shrunk toward zero with penalty λ = 30, so a category with few live comparables is pulled toward the global instead of trusting a thin centre. The level factor (0.85) is fitted leave-one-out from the same comparables, so the median comparable is priced at its actual market cap. At $100M of TVL the fitted multiple is ×0.135 after the level factor. Nothing here is a hard-coded list of names, and a protocol that records a token already is never ranked: it is shown apart, at the market’s own value.
